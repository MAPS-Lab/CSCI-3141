# Foundations of Data Science

CSCI 3141 · Dalhousie University

Welcome to the public course materials for Foundations of Data Science. Use
this collection to follow a lecture, revisit a method, or work through examples
independently at your own pace.

The course follows a data science project from its first question through data
understanding, preparation, modelling, evaluation, and communication. You will
practise making analytical choices and explaining reproducible results.

## Start here

1. **Read the syllabus.** It sets the learning outcomes, assessment requirements,
   submission rules, and course policies. If you are enrolled, check that its
   course offering matches the one identified by your instructor.
2. **Open a class's slides and notes together.** The slides show the main ideas,
   figures, and examples. The notes provide fuller explanations and worked
   steps to revisit after class.
3. **Try the examples yourself.** Run the code, compare your output with the
   explanation, and change an input to see what happens. Bring your questions
   to class.

## Work in R or Python

R is the primary teaching language, with Python examples provided alongside it
where appropriate. Integrated labs accept either language under the same
requirements and rubric.

Choose one working environment and practise running an analysis from start to
finish. You can work locally or use a cloud notebook option described in the
syllabus.

## Build your data science portfolio

Labs and assignments build a record of your progress. A useful portfolio explains
the question, identifies the data, shows your reasoning, and tells a reader how
to reproduce the results.

For assessed work, follow this sequence and the detailed syllabus instructions.

1. **Develop privately.** Give the instructor access to your private work
   repository and commit regularly. Keep cloud notebooks and links that reveal
   answers private too.
2. **Submit a fixed version.** Send the full commit SHA and its commit URL through
   the announced private channel by the deadline. Preserve the submitted commit
   and its history; make corrections in new commits rather than rewriting or
   backdating earlier work.
3. **Share when released.** After the deadline and when the instructor opens
   sharing, publish that same submitted commit and its history in your portfolio
   and present that version in class. Label later improvements separately.

The syllabus explains the submission evidence and alternatives when privacy,
accommodation, safety, copyright, or licensing requires a private portfolio.
Assessed work belongs in your own repository and the announced submission
channel, not in this teaching-materials repository.
